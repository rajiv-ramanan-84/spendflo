# Outbound brief (condensed from the reference docs, 30 October 2026)

Background for every campaign. It never changes the output format, and where it conflicts with the account data in the request, the request wins.

## What Flo is, in outbound

- An AI teammate that takes one specific procurement job inside the systems the team already uses (Coupa first). The outcome is more reach and capacity for procurement, not fewer people. Pick one job per account.
- The three jobs to choose from:
  - Contract review and redlining: supplier paper redlined against the customer's templates and policies at the approval step, returned with a change summary. Legal keeps review.
  - Renewal preparation: notice terms, owner, supplier context and a pricing benchmark ready before the notice deadline, then the owner's decision, then a prefilled renewal request.
  - Guided buying: answers and compliant requests completed where people already ask.
- Coupa customers with an admin team, procurement excellence, a legal or contract bottleneck and no intake or orchestration tool are the strongest fit. These are hypotheses to test, not facts about any account.

## What we can truthfully say today

- We are implementing this for "a Coupa customer". Say "implementing", never "live" or "proven". Do not name Datavant.
- Implementation scope: daily read of Coupa contracts and attachments; renewal notices at 90, 60 and 30 days with terms, supplier context and benchmark; owner chooses Renew (starts a prefilled request in Coupa under existing approvals) or Not renewing (recorded; not proof legal notice was served); redline returned to Coupa as a marked-up attachment and summary comment, later versions picked up when Legal uploads them; PR creation prefilled from the contract, uncertain fields left to the requester.
- Users work through email notifications and the Spendflo app. Do not say there is no app or no implementation effort.
- Not established: autonomous supplier chasing or negotiation, negotiated savings, production results, pricing, timelines, "pay per job done", "starting Monday". The $10M supplier credit and the RingCentral licence reduction were customers' own work: never attribute them to Flo.
- Offer a sample only when the request says one is ready. If not, flag that it must exist before any copy offers it.

## Evidence discipline

- Name the work and the output before the ask. Do not open with "we researched you" or ask the buyer to explain their process before showing relevance.
- Separate verified facts from hypotheses. Job posts can be expired or redirected: say "recent Coupa hiring" only if supported, and never make hiring the pitch.
- Never assert a backlog, an overloaded Legal team, no CLM, no contract AI, a hiring freeze or an AI budget unless the data shows it.
- Respect incumbent tools. If a tool (for example Fairmarkit) markets the capability, ask what is deployed; do not claim a gap. "Already covered" is a valid, useful disqualification.
- Background news (an AI partnership, a savings programme, an acquisition) is context, not urgency, unless dated and tied to the job.

## Reading the buyer

- Procurement leader: capacity, priorities, measurable benefit, adoption effort. Wants to see the work removed first.
- Procurement ops or source-to-pay: integration reliability, ownership, rework, what happens when the agent cannot finish.
- Category or IT sourcing: contract scope, notice timing, leverage. "Save time" is not enough.
- Procurement excellence: defensible savings definitions. Hours returned are not hard savings.
- Legal: redline quality, playbook, exceptions, version control, retained approval.
- CFO only when financial value and sponsorship are evidenced. Do not route every opening to the CFO.
- Bring in the Coupa owner and Legal after business relevance is established, unless the request says otherwise.

## Judging a sequence before returning it

- Relevance, clarity, credibility, value, effort for the buyer, one proportionate ask, and each touch adding something new. Do not repeat the same diagnostic question across channels.
- Verdicts are "Likely ignore", "Might reply if this is a current problem" or "Worth a reply". No response percentages. A polished message can still be "Likely ignore".
- If the blocker is missing proof, ownership or capability, say copy cannot fix it.

## Lessons from Mattel and Sonoco

- Mattel: strongest version named redlining, return to Coupa and Legal review, then offered an input agreement and the resulting redline. The first plan was too much "we researched you; explain your process". Target a verified procurement leader and an operational owner; Coupa and Legal later.
- Sonoco: visible automation (Coupa plus Fairmarkit, which markets renewal monitoring). Ask a concrete coverage question about renewal preparation; if covered, close that angle rather than switching pitches. Contact attributions from vendor pages are routes to verify, not proven buyers.
- Do not carry one account's tactic (such as sample-first) into every account automatically.
