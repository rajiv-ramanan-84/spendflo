# Procurement persona response review

Use a composite buyer simulation, never impersonation or a claim of actual customer feedback. Derived from Siddharth's Finance and Procurement persona and this conversation's reviews. Adapt to the account evidence: the source persona's generic “rejected Coupa,” weak PO process, overwhelmed staff and AI mandate are not facts about Mattel, Sonoco or every enterprise.

## Choose the reader

- Procurement leader: capacity, sourcing priorities, measurable benefit, adoption effort and a credible owner. Wants to see the work removed before explaining the whole operation to a stranger.
- Procurement operations / Source to Pay: integration reliability, workflow ownership, current-system fit, rework and change-management burden. Ask what happens when the agent cannot complete the task.
- Category manager / IT sourcing: contract scope, owner requirements, notice timing, commercial leverage and supplier context. Generic “save time” is insufficient.
- Procurement excellence: defensible savings definitions, standardization and observable process improvements. Do not equate hours returned with hard savings.
- Legal/contracts: redline quality, playbook application, exception handling, version control and retained approval. Summaries alone do not establish trustworthy review.
- CFO, when relevant: evidenced financial value, implementation burden, accountable sponsorship and controls. Do not route every operational opening to the CFO.

## Test the message

Judge the work, not merely the prose:
1. Relevance: Does this reader own the job? Is the pain observed or clearly hypothesized?
2. Clarity: Can the buyer identify the action, output and retained decision?
3. Credibility: Is there accurate evidence and capability status? Does the copy recognize incumbent coverage without invented limitations?
4. Value: Is there a reason to investigate beyond “AI” or “less work”? Do not invent quantified outcomes to fill a gap.
5. Effort: Is the buyer being asked to educate the vendor, fund an uncertain build or enter a transformation project?
6. Ask: One clear step proportional to trust. A sample request is not a meeting commitment or buying intent.
7. Sequence: Does each touch add something? Do not merely repeat a diagnostic question across email, LinkedIn and calls.

## Give an honest verdict

Use “Likely ignore,” “Might reply if this is a current problem,” or “Worth a reply.” Explain the conditions; do not assign unsupported response percentages. Give a short simulated internal reaction in the buyer's voice and the most plausible response, such as “Send the sample,” “We already cover this,” or a referral. A polished message may still deserve “Likely ignore.”

For a full plan, identify the weakest touch and fix it; for a persona review, critique each material touch when useful. Revise at most twice in one pass. If the blocker is missing proof, ownership or capability, state that copy alone cannot solve it. Do not automatically upgrade the verdict after a rewrite.

## Lessons from the actual review

Mattel's first sequence led with job-posting research and repeated discovery questions. Simulated buyer reaction: the vendor asks me to map my process before demonstrating enough relevance. “AI teammate” was too abstract. The stronger version named redlining, return to Coupa and Legal review, then offered an input agreement and resulting redline. Expected first response was at most “send it over,” conditional on current pain. This was a simulated assessment, not measured buyer response.

Sonoco already has a visible automation investment and Fairmarkit markets renewal capabilities. A concrete coverage question is more defensible than asserting renewal chaos or offering another broad automation platform. If the process is covered, accept the answer. Do not carry Mattel's sample-first tactic into every account automatically.

If product proof is missing, recommend preparing a realistic sample. Do not manufacture customer outputs or call illustrative screens a live deployment. Keep critique and research notes outside send-ready messages.
