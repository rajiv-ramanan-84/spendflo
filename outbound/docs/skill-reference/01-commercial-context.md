# Flo commercial context

Snapshot: 30 October 2026. Sources: Siddharth's pasted GTM retro and linked internal vision pitch, last modified 20 October 2026. Read the originals for detail; refresh connected documents when current precision matters.

## Positioning
Flo is an AI teammate that takes a specific procurement job inside existing working channels and systems. The strategic outcome is greater procurement reach and capacity. Lead with work the team cannot get to; do not default to headcount replacement. The internal vision spans many jobs; outbound should select one relevant job.

The pitch's “pay per job done,” “starting Monday,” and broad autonomy language require confirmation before external use. Product vision does not establish production readiness. The workload-growth statistic has no supporting citation in the pitch and should not become an external fact without verification.

The $10m supplier-credit finding and RingCentral licence reduction/$1.2m credit describe customers' own activities. Do not attribute them to Flo or assume permission to name customers. There is no owned manufacturing proof point established by the pitch. Avoid universal claims that suites never act or that competitors lack capabilities; establish the actual workflow gap.

The retro describes procurement leaders, contract managers, procurement operations, procurement excellence, transformation and suite administrators as relevant roles. CPO/VP procurement may sponsor; operational owners diagnose the job; legal, security, IT and finance validate their domains. Establish roles rather than assuming title equals authority.

Coupa is a strong starting segment, especially with an admin team and procurement excellence, a legal/contract bottleneck, existing PR-to-PO process and no intake/orchestration product. Ariba also appears in contract notes. Guided-buying hypotheses include large requester populations, public AI initiatives and a new S2P implementation. Renewal hypotheses include high software-contract volume and thin procurement staffing; the retro suggests $100m revenue per procurement FTE as a threshold. These are unvalidated targeting hypotheses, not universal gates. Do not claim guaranteed wins or retention from “weird not to buy.”

## Lessons to retain
Specific projects per persona, founder outreach, advisor introductions and real shared context worked. Customized demos and practical implementation knowledge built credibility. Generic positioning and unsupported systems expertise damaged it. Include product/engineering in technical qualification where needed; name integration unknowns.

Workshops attracted engaged attendees but follow-up was weak. Registration is not attendance; send relevant recordings only when available and use the actual engagement context. Give follow-up an owner, next action and date. POCs need agreed scope, baseline, success criteria, access requirements, human checkpoints and a decision date.

The retro favors North America events, concentrated sponsorship and private dinners over scattered attendance. Treat this as a planning preference to revisit, not proof that every event will work. Separate workshop learning and validation from qualified pipeline.
